# Risk register

| ID | Risk | Severity | Mitigation / acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---:|---|
| R01 | A worker without one of the approved 12-month templates is accidentally treated as an in-scope progressive-PIT employee. | critical | Scope decision: the MVP supports only the supplied full-time and part-time 12-month contracts. Fail closed for missing, separate-probation, under-three-month, or materially different contracts. |
| R13 | A part-time worker is always exempted from insurance, or eligibility is decided once instead of from that month’s wage and effective threshold. | critical | Evaluate each fund per month; test below, exactly at, and above the effective threshold, including VND 2,530,000 from 1 July 2026. |
| R14 | Personal-Zalo automation or an ineligible OA message route leaks credentials, violates platform rules, is blocked, or delivers a payslip to an unverified Zalo identity. | critical | Personal Zalo is manual handoff only with operator confirmation and no stored session secrets. Automated delivery requires a verified OA/OpenAPI route, recipient binding/consent, exact preview, attachment capability and limits, idempotency, quota handling, and immutable provider receipts. Keep Gmail as the independent primary channel. |
| R02 | A generic 400% holiday formula double-pays monthly workers or underpays another wage form. | critical | Model paid-holiday entitlement and overtime premium separately; test monthly, hourly/daily, and non-worked holiday cases. |
| R03 | One `gross` or one `insurance_base` silently conflates distinct fund bases and component eligibility. | critical | Per-component/per-fund classification matrix and independently capped calculation traces. |
| R04 | A rule is selected by application date instead of earning period, payment date, tax period, or correction context. | critical | Introduce a four-date rule-resolution contract and freeze the selected pack on each finalized item. |
| R05 | Email batch mistakes disclose one employee’s salary to another. | critical | Recipient preview, one-message/one-recipient invariant, idempotency, two-person release, synthetic end-to-end isolation tests, and provider receipt. |
| R06 | A highly generic rules engine makes payroll behavior opaque and hard to certify. | high | Prefer typed domain policy modules plus signed tables; expose a human-readable calculation trace. |
| R07 | Editing a published legal rule mutates historical payroll results. | high | Immutable, content-addressed rule packs; corrections create new versions and adjustment runs. |
| R08 | The product implies annual PIT finalization or statutory filing while implementing only monthly withholding. | high | Name the boundary explicitly in UI and contracts; block exports that are not implemented and reviewed. |
| R09 | Personal data is retained forever or deleted despite statutory accounting obligations. | high | Record-class retention schedule, legal holds, deletion queue, evidence log, and periodic review. |
| R10 | Cloud email, object storage, observability, or identity services create unreviewed cross-border processing. | high | Data-flow map, vendor/subprocessor register, hosting decision, contracts, and legal review before production data. |
| R11 | A ten-week calendar is treated as a guarantee despite unknown team capacity and unmeasured implementation/runtime work. | medium | U01-U05 are resolved at the design layer, but estimate only from the approved architecture and task graph; use acceptance-gated milestones rather than date-only phases. |
| R12 | Docker adds operational work without improving the first internal deployment. | medium | Choose no container for the pure engine/local validation; use Docker only for a reproducible stateful app deployment with documented ports, mounts, backups, and cleanup. |
| R15 | A static employee QR can be photographed or forwarded, allowing another person to clock in, or can expose personal data/reusable credentials. | critical | Use an opaque random identifier only, bind acceptance to an authorized shop device, require a second employee factor such as PIN, preserve raw events, and send every correction through review/audit. Never encode PII, server address, or a reusable bearer credential. |
| R16 | Publishing the home payroll server directly to the Internet exposes payroll and attendance data or makes payroll availability depend on an unsafe router configuration. | critical | No direct router port-forwarding. Prefer a private device-to-device route for a managed shop kiosk; if a public employee-phone flow is later approved, isolate a minimal attendance ingress from the payroll UI/database and apply strong authentication, rate limiting, revocation, monitoring, and security review. |
| R17 | A remembered personal-phone session is copied, stolen, or remains active after the employee changes or loses the device. | high | Owner approval before enrollment; high-entropy session token in a secure cookie; hashed server-side storage; rotation, expiry, per-device inventory, immediate revocation, employment-status revocation, and mandatory live shop QR challenge for each event. Do not treat browser/device fingerprinting as authentication. |
| R18 | Internet/tunnel/home-server failure causes a missed punch, or an ambiguous timeout is shown as success and later duplicates on retry. | high | Online-only fail-closed UI: acknowledge only after durable server acceptance, show `not recorded` for failures, use idempotency keys for retries, and route missed punches through accountant-created, owner-approved corrections with reasons and immutable audit history. |
| R19 | A planned shift or store opening hours are mistaken for proof of work, or excess time is automatically treated as payable overtime without approval. | critical | Keep schedule, raw attendance, approved work segments, and payroll classifications separate. Generate exceptions for review; only an approved attendance-period snapshot may feed payroll, and holiday/overtime classifications remain explicit and traceable. |
| R20 | The engine subtracts a default meal/rest break even though this employer pays all breaks, underpaying hourly staff. | critical | Store the paid-break policy explicitly, require zero automatic break deduction, omit break punches from the employee flow, and test full-time and part-time shifts of six and ten hours. Any future unpaid-break policy is a separately reviewed effective-dated change. |
| R21 | The current VND 26,000 hourly rate is accepted forever even though it is only VND 500 above the Region I floor of VND 25,500 effective from 1 January 2026, or a later rate change overwrites historical payroll. | critical | Validate every effective-dated compensation term against the workplace/rule version for the earning period; warn before saving, block below-floor values, preserve old terms, and add boundary tests at VND 25,499, VND 25,500, and VND 26,000 plus a future-floor change. |
| R22 | A gross contractual salary/rate is displayed or paid as if it were guaranteed take-home pay, or employee deductions are applied twice. | critical | Fix one MVP compensation basis (`gross`), label inputs and contract imports clearly, compute deductions once from classified components, show the full gross-to-net trace on screen and payslip, and block net/gross-up configurations. |
| R23 | The local `G:` backup is mistaken for complete disaster recovery, automation overwrites unrelated files, or the only encryption recovery secret is lost with the PC/drive. | critical | Isolate writes to `G:\PaySlip-Backups`, refuse path escape, encrypt and hash each set, use retention and capacity checks, and prove restore before real payroll data. Never touch existing `G:` content outside the dedicated directory. Keep a sealed paper recovery copy physically separate and never store the secret inside the backup. Continue to report theft/fire/ransomware/offsite exposure until a future encrypted cloud or physically rotated offsite copy is verified. |
| R24 | “Simple” removes necessary payroll evidence, or “premium” becomes decoration that slows the main workflows. | high | Use progressive disclosure rather than omission; preserve full calculation/source/audit details behind plain-language summaries. Enforce one primary action, bounded step counts, keyboard/touch accessibility, responsive and visual-regression tests, and moderated task checks with owner/accountant users. |
| R25 | Codex or Hermes completes a packet in prose while changing extra files, skipping verification, or treating static evidence as runtime proof. | high | Assign stable task IDs and exact writable paths; require structured handoffs, changed-file checks, test receipts, evidence-layer labels, and deterministic stop conditions. Codex independently reviews critical artifacts and reruns focused verification before integration. |
